What is a Computer Network?

Understanding how devices connect and communicate

Definition

A computer network is a system in which two or more computers are connected together to share data, files, and resources such as printers, storage, and internet access. Networks allow devices to communicate with each other using cables, Wi-Fi, or other transmission media.

Types of Computer Networks

1. LAN (Local Area Network)

A LAN connects computers within a small area such as a home, school, or office. It usually covers a distance of a few meters to a couple of kilometers and offers high speed with low cost.

2. MAN (Metropolitan Area Network)

A MAN covers a larger area than a LAN, such as a city or a town. It is often used to connect multiple LANs together, for example linking branch offices of a company across a city.

3. WAN (Wide Area Network)

A WAN covers a very large geographical area, such as a country or even the whole world. The Internet is the biggest and most common example of a WAN, connecting millions of networks globally.

Advantages of Computer Networks

  • Easy and fast sharing of data between devices
  • Resource sharing such as printers, files, and storage
  • Centralized data management and backup
  • Access to the internet and online communication tools
  • Better collaboration between users and teams

Disadvantages of Computer Networks

  • Security risks such as hacking and unauthorized access
  • High setup and maintenance cost
  • Viruses and malware can spread quickly across connected devices
  • Network failure can disrupt work for all connected users
💡 Tip: Networks are usually classified by the area they cover (LAN, MAN, WAN), but they can also be classified by how they are connected (wired vs wireless) or by their architecture (client-server vs peer-to-peer).

Conclusion

Computer networks are an essential part of modern life. They make communication faster, data sharing easier, and connect the world through the internet. Understanding networking basics is the first step towards learning more advanced topics like the OSI model.
